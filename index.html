The rapid rise of image generation calls for detection methods that are both interpretable and reliable. Existing approaches, though accurate, act as black boxes and fail to generalize to out-of-distribution data, while multi-modal large language models (MLLMs) provide reasoning ability but often hallucinate. To address these issues, we construct FakeXplained dataset of AI-generated images annotated with bounding boxes and descriptive captions that highlight synthesis artifacts, forming the basis for human-aligned, visually grounded reasoning. Leveraging FakeXplained, we develop FakeXplainer which fine-tunes MLLMs with a progressive training pipeline, enabling accurate detection, artifact localization, and coherent textual explanations. Extensive experiments show that FakeXplainer not only sets a new state-of-the-art in detection and localization accuracy (98.2% accuracy, 36.0% IoU), but also demonstrates strong robustness and out-of-distribution generalization, uniquely delivering spatially grounded, human-aligned rationales.

  • ✓ Pairs spatial bounding boxes directly with fine-grained artifact caption explanations.
  • ✓ Solves the MLLM hallucination bottleneck through progressive supervised alignment.

Abstract

AI-generated images have become increasingly photorealistic, making robust and interpretable forensic analysis more challenging under generator shift and post-processing. AI-generated images have become increasingly photorealistic, making robust and interpretable forensic analysis more challenging under generator shift and post-processing. Existing methods often rely on a single source of evidence, such as high-level semantic cues or low-level artifact patterns, which limits their generalization when image distributions change. In this paper, we present EXACT, a unified dual-evidence framework for AI-generated image forensics that jointly supports detection, source attribution, and pixel-level localization. EXACT combines two complementary branches: a Semantic Branch based on a CLIP encoder to capture content-level inconsistencies, and a Trace Branch that leverages Haar wavelet sub-bands and a Transformer to model low-level artifact traces. To improve robustness across images and generators, we further introduce a lightweight fusion module that predicts adaptive per-image weights to balance the two evidences. In addition, a frozen Large Multimodal Model is used to generate concise natural-language forensic reports from model outputs, improving the interpretability of the analysis. Experiments show that EXACT achieves 99.49% detection accuracy on the in-domain test set and strong cross-dataset generalization. Across the extended cross-dataset evaluation, it obtains an average accuracy of 84.88% and an average precision of 94.18%. EXACT also provides reliable attribution performance and meaningful localization results.

  • ✓ Synthesizes CLIP semantics with high-frequency Haar wavelet sub-bands.
  • ✓ Adaptive per-image weighting prevents over-reliance on a single failure mode.

Abstract

The misuse of AI imagery can have harmful societal effects, prompting the creation of detectors to combat issues like the spread of fake news. Existing methods can effectively detect images generated by seen generators, but it is challenging to detect those generated by unseen generators. They do not concentrate on amplifying the output discrepancy when detectors process real versus fake images. This results in a close output distribution of real and fake samples, increasing classification difficulty in detecting unseen generators. This paper addresses the unseen-generator detection problem by considering this task from the perspective of anomaly detection and proposes an adversarial teacher-student discrepancy-aware framework. Our method encourages smaller output discrepancies between the student and the teacher models for real images while aiming for larger discrepancies for fake images. We employ adversarial learning to train a feature augmenter, which promotes smaller discrepancies between teacher and student networks when the inputs are fake images. Our method has achieved state-of-the-art on public benchmarks, and the visualization results show that a large output discrepancy is maintained when faced with various types of generators.

  • ✓ Amplifies student-teacher divergence exclusively on synthetic generation inputs.
  • ✓ Employs adversarial feature augmenters to prevent memorization of seen generators.

The rapid development of generative models (Diffusion Models and GANs) has enabled synthesizing photorealistic images indistinguishable from real ones to human observers. However, existing datasets for detecting AI-generated images suffer from limited size, lack of generative diversity, and narrow domain coverage. We introduce GenImage, an ultra-large-scale benchmark consisting of over 1.33 million images divided into real and synthetic pairs across 1,000 ImageNet categories. GenImage encompasses 8 representative generative models including Stable Diffusion (v1.4, v1.5), Midjourney, GLIDE, VQDM, Wukong, and ADM. Extensive evaluations across modern vision backbones reveal critical cross-generator generalization gaps and provide standard benchmark protocols for the synthetic image forensic community.

Abstract

Detecting diffusion-generated images is challenging because their artifacts differ drastically from traditional GAN artifacts. We propose DIRE (Diffusion Reconstruction Error), based on the hypothesis that synthetic diffusion images can be reconstructed by pre-trained diffusion models with significantly lower error...

Detecting images generated by diffusion models (e.g., DDPM, Stable Diffusion, Imagen) presents unique challenges due to subtle artifact patterns that differ drastically from traditional GAN checkerboard artifacts. We propose DIRE (Diffusion Reconstruction Error), a novel detection representation based on the hypothesis that synthetic diffusion images can be reconstructed by an off-the-shelf pre-trained diffusion model with significantly lower reconstruction error than real images. By feeding an input image through a deterministic DDIM inversion and reconstruction pipeline, the pixel-wise difference image (DIRE) acts as an explicit forensic fingerprint. Experiments demonstrate state-of-the-art detection accuracy and remarkable robustness against JPEG compression and blurring.

  • ✓ Leverages mathematical inversion disparity in latent diffusion denoising steps.
  • ✓ Supplements frequency-space traces when high-frequency spectrum has been washed out.

Abstract

As synthetic media generation becomes ubiquitous, heuristic neural classifiers alone cannot provide complete mathematical certainty without cryptographic provenance. The C2PA Standard defines an open, verifiable specification for binding content credentials directly into media assets...

As generative AI capabilities continue to accelerate, heuristic neural classifiers alone cannot provide complete mathematical certainty without cryptographic provenance. The C2PA Standard defines an open, verifiable technical specification for binding content credentials, author identity, capture hardware telemetry, and editing lineage into digital assets via cryptographically signed JUMBF (ISO/IEC 19566-5) metadata manifests. Any unauthorized pixel alteration or re-generation without a co-signed credential invalidates the tamper-evident signature chain, delivering verifiable trust from sensor acquisition to viewer display.
